Anatomy of a
Flock camera

Flock Safety reports more than 120,000 license plate reader cameras in 49 states. This page documents one Falcon unit at scale: the pole and mount, each internal component, the power and network connections, the data path from capture to deletion, common claims against the public record, and pricing and contract terms.

Sources are Flock Safety's specification sheets, price lists and data-architecture document, three independent teardowns, audit logs released under public records requests, and court and congressional records. Each figure carries a citation. Statements from Flock and from independent sources are labelled separately; where they differ, both are cited.

01 · Pole and mount

Pole, mount and field of view

Flock's standard installation is a dedicated breakaway pole set in soil by a Flock crew. The camera is band-clamped at about ten feet and aimed diagonally along the lane so that the rear plates of departing vehicles pass through the field of view. No conductor runs to grade: power is supplied by the pole-top solar array and data is transmitted over LTE. Two other mount configurations are documented below.

02 · Inside the enclosure

Components, front to back

The enclosure separates at its seam. Inside are a Qualcomm APQ8053 system on module running Android 8.1, a five-megapixel infrared-optimised image sensor, a ring of six 850 nm emitters, an LTE module, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, GPS, and a passive infrared trigger. Fourteen components are listed. Select a component to isolate it.

03 · Power and cable

Power and network connections

The Falcon has no wired network interface and is not a Power over Ethernet device. In the solar configuration the conductors on the pole carry DC from the solar array and the external battery box to the camera's rear connector. In the AC configuration a licensed electrician terminates 120 V mains in a junction box and Flock's AC kit converts it to DC.

Fiber, SFP modules and PoE are used in one configuration: Flock Wing, a gateway appliance installed in a network closet that ingests video from an agency's existing cameras and runs plate recognition on it. That configuration is modelled as the third path.

04 · Data path · capture to deletion

Data path in twelve stages

A vehicle triggers the motion sensor, the image sensor captures six to twelve frames, the on-device model reads the plate and classifies the vehicle, and the result is transmitted over LTE. The twelve stages below trace one detection through Flock's cloud, hot-list matching, network search, audit logging and scheduled deletion, with the processing location stated at each stage.
